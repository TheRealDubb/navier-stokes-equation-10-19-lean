A Lean-Verified Alternative Proof of the Pressure-Flux Estimate in Navier–Stokes Lemma 10.5

Troy Winarski

Human research director · ORCID 0009-0004-5956-3825

Abstract

OpenAI’s written proof of (10.19) estimates a local pressure term in L3/2. The corresponding upstream Lean development bounds the physical flux by an expression that also involves localized velocity dissipation. We give a companion Lean proof of the printed velocity-only bound. A second cutoff commutation moves two further powers of the cutoff onto the stress tensor. The resulting local pairing admits an L² estimate, while both commutator errors retain the required decay in the radius. The argument allows an essential-in-time energy bound for the competing velocity. Its conclusion holds at every interior time, with one constant for the whole slab. [1, 2, 8]

The physical estimate

Let u, v be the reference and competing velocities on ℝ³, with respective pressures p, P. Write w = v − u and π = P − p. Choose a fixed smooth compactly supported φ with 0 ≤ φ ≤ 1 and φ = 1 on the unit ball. At a radius R set

ρ(x) = φ(x/R), χR = ρ8, BR(t) = ‖ρ4w(t)‖6.

Under the slab hypotheses on page 2, there is CT ≥ 0 such that for every R ≥ 1 and every 0 < t < T, the following holds with B = BR(t).

|∫ℝ³ π(t,x) w(t,x) · ∇χR(x) dx|
 ≤ (CT/R) [(B + 1)B1/2 + R−3/4B3/4]. (10.19)

The right-hand side uses the fourth-power weighted L⁶ velocity norm at the same radius as the physical cutoff. The manuscript asserts the inequality for almost every interior time. The companion yields it for each such time. Appendix A gives the estimates and an explicit admissible constant.

1. Formal statement and hypotheses

Fix T > 0. Both velocities and pressures are smooth on the closed slab [0,T] × ℝ³. Both velocities have zero divergence and the same viscosity-one Navier–Stokes residual on the interior, expressing equality of the applied force. One compact set contains the spatial supports of u at all slab times. The spatial L² norm of v has a finite essential bound in time. All spatial norms and integrals use Lebesgue volume.

Reading the signature

The spatial Fréchet derivative fderiv of ρ⁸ applied to v − u is the directional derivative w · ∇χR. The premise hev puts L² membership and a uniform bound on the same full-measure set of times. The conclusion chooses CT before quantifying over R and t. The proof uses neither an initial-data equality nor a magnitude bound on the competing pressure.

The zero case is also checked separately. If B = 0, the weighted velocity vanishes almost everywhere. At such points either ρ = 0 or w = 0, so the physical integrand vanishes. This follows for any pressure from PaperFluxZero.integrand_ae_zero.

2. Prior work and numerical comparison

Written estimates and the reported discrepancy

OpenAI states (10.19) after commuting the fourth-power cutoff through the double Riesz transform and estimating the local term in L3/2 [1]. KokunoYumeto’s pinned workbench records the same normalized expression as (E.21) [3]. Open Maths derives the quantitative bound before absorption into CT in its re-audit [4]. Bastounis, Circelli and Hansen identify the change between the printed argument and the upstream Lean estimate, whose right-hand side also contains the localized gradient norm AR [2, §3.2].

Related formalizations

MathGraph proves a bound for a legacy canonical weighted-pressure pairing and develops a transpose identity [5]. Its polynomial upper bound is proportional to R−1(B3/2 + B + 1). Specializing the underlying raw bound at R = L = M = V = 1 leaves a nonnegative contribution of 16 for every B. The pairing also requires an identification with the physical flux. The later MathGraph comparison work records (10.19) as open [5].

Bourakkadi’s Layer 7 verifies conditional scalar scaling, absorption and cancellation identities relevant to the comparison [6]. Hunt reports a formal estimate using enlarged cutoff radii and weighted dissipation [7]. His support example concerns radius dilations. For the original powers, the zero-case lemma on page 2 gives a vanishing physical integrand whenever B = 0.

A scalar test for numerical implication

Fix the radius at one and take B(t) = t⁴, F(t) = t on 0 < t < 1. These scalar data meet the raw upper bound above. For any proposed constant C ≥ 0, the target right-hand side is bounded by

C[(1 + t⁴)t² + t³] ≤ 3Ct².

Thus F(t) exceeds the target on an interval of positive length for every fixed C. This establishes failure of numerical domination even with a null exceptional set of times. Additional arguments using the fields or their equations could still yield (10.19). The continuous-family calculation is analytic. The separate audit theorem PressureReview.no_uniform_scalar_implication kernel-checks the positive scalar witness for each C.

The contribution offered here is the attached proof for the physical, normalized estimate with a second commutator. The public-source search found earlier written derivations and related Lean results. Historical priority for this argument or an exact formalization remains unresolved.

Appendix A. From the physical flux to three pairings

A.1. Pressure recovery on a time slice

Fix 0 < t < T and R ≥ 1. Write w = v − u, π = P − p, ρ(x) = φ(x/R), χR = ρ8 and B = ‖ρ4w‖6. The fixed smooth compact cutoff satisfies 0 ≤ φ ≤ 1 and equals one on the unit ball. Put L ≥ ‖∇φ‖∞ and a = 8L. The chain rule gives ∇χR = 8ρ7∇ρ and ‖∇ρ‖∞ ≤ L/R.

For Dij = RiRj, put gij = uiuj − vivj and ψk = 8ρk∇ρ · (u − v). The pairing ⟨f,h⟩ denotes ∫ fh dx. Testing the recovered pressure gradient against the compact vector field ρ8(u − v) and using zero divergence gives

F = ∫ π w · ∇χR dx = Re ∑i,j ⟨gij, Dijψ7⟩.

Both pressure and velocity differences reverse signs in the upstream convention. A spatially constant pressure correction contributes zero because the test field is compactly supported and w is divergence-free.

A.2. Two exact decompositions and the local estimate

Suppress i,j. Define E(h,f,ψ) = ⟨f,D(hψ) − hDψ⟩. Using ψ7 = ρ4ψ3 and ψ3 = ρ²ψ1 gives the following two identities.

⟨g,Dψ7⟩ = ⟨ρ4g,Dψ3⟩ + E(ρ4,g,ψ3),
⟨ρ4g,Dψ3⟩ = ⟨ρ6g,Dψ1⟩ + E(ρ²,ρ4g,ψ1).

Energy gives g ∈ L¹. The localized tensors are continuous with compact support, hence lie in L6/5. Their products with transformed Schwartz tests are integrable by Hölder. For the local term, take M ≥ ‖w‖2 and Uq ≥ ‖u‖q. Since ρ⁶|g| ≤ (ρ²|w|)(ρ⁴|w| + 2|u|), Hölder with exponents 3 and 6 and interpolation in A.3 give

‖ρ6g‖2 ≤ M1/2B1/2(B + 2U6), ‖ψ1‖2 ≤ aM/R.

The double-Riesz multiplier has L² operator norm at most one. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the local pairing by the product of these two quantities.

Appendix A. Commutator bounds and uniformity

A.3. The two commutator bounds

Weighted interpolation uses the endpoints ‖w‖2 ≤ M and ‖ρ4w‖6 = B. Pointwise, ρ²|w| = |w|1/2(ρ⁴|w|)1/2 and ρ³|w| = |w|1/4(ρ⁴|w|)3/4. Hölder and 0 ≤ ρ ≤ 1 give

‖ρ3w‖3 ≤ ‖ρ²w‖3 ≤ M1/2B1/2, ‖ρ3w‖4 ≤ M1/4B3/4.

Thus ‖ψ3‖4 ≤ (a/R)M1/4B3/4. Applying Hölder to ρ3|w| and ρ(|w| + 2|u|) gives ‖ρ4g‖4/3 ≤ M1/4B3/4(M + 2U2). The heat representation bounds the absolute commutator kernel by a cutoff-dependent multiple of HR(z) = |z|−3 min(|z|/R,1). Scaling in three dimensions gives ‖HR‖4/3 = R−3/4‖H1‖4/3.

Set K1 = k max(4L,1) and K2 = k max(2L,1), where k is the universal Riesz commutator constant. For measurable cutoffs ρ² and ρ, the Lipschitz bounds are 2L/R and L/R. The squared-cutoff estimates therefore give

|E(ρ4,g,ψ3)| ≤ K1R−3/4 ‖g‖1 ‖ψ3‖4,
|E(ρ²,ρ4g,ψ1)| ≤ K2R−3/4 ‖ρ4g‖4/3 ‖ψ1‖2.

The first convolution maps L¹ into L4/3 with kernel HR. In the second, two measurable L4/3 factors convolve into L². The smooth compact tests also belong to L⁴ as required in the heat representation. Both remainders acquire the additional R−1 from ∇ρ in their test.

A.4. One constant for the whole slab

At each t ∈ [0,T] choose good times tending to t where ‖v‖2 ≤ Mv. Slab continuity gives spatial pointwise convergence. Fatou applied to squared velocities yields ∫|v(t,x)|² dx ≤ Mv², including the endpoints. Continuity and common compact support bound U2,U6 uniformly. Energy then supplies uniform M and G ≥ maxij ‖gij‖1.

Sum the three bounds and the nine tensor components. Using B + 2U6 ≤ (1 + 2U6)(B + 1), the following choice gives (10.19) for every R ≥ 1 and every interior t.

CT = 9 max{aM3/2(1 + 2U6), aM1/4[K1G + K2M(M + 2U2)]}.
